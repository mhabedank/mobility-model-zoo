# Fable review 1 of the scout guideline v3 brief

Date: 2026-11-28. Reviewed: `brief.yaml`, `examples.yaml`, `brief-provenance.md`, `brief-questions.md` and the listed context documents. Method: one lead reviewer with seven subagents (two for web research, five with one analysis lens each). No file under `data/` was read, no real text was labeled, no output of a labeling model was judged, and no existing file was changed. Everything in sections 4 to 7 is a proposal; the owner decides. Proposed wordings are Claude drafts and need the owner's approval under constitution 3.2.0 like any other guideline text.

## 1. Summary

**Verdict: not ready for the pilot.** The definitions are a sound base, and most problems can be fixed with wording and example corrections in one round. But as written, the pilot would produce numbers that cannot be interpreted, and several fields would be dropped for arithmetic reasons instead of real disagreement. Findings: 6 blockers, 14 major, 11 minor.

Top 5 risks:

1. **No operational item-boundary rule.** The only rule is one German sentence; the examples cut, merge and split inconsistently. Item-matching F1 would measure framing habits (R-01).
2. **Job, gain and plain activity are not separable.** The definitions overlap and the examples label the same pattern both ways (R-02).
3. **The pilot metrics are named but not defined**: matching criterion, unit of analysis, empty handling, statistic for list fields, "30 units", pilot size (R-03).
4. **Links cannot pass.** End-to-end link F1 is capped near 0.3 to 0.4 by item matching, the threshold equals the floor, and positives are too rare for 30 units (R-04).
5. **The examples contradict written rules** for `states_solution`, absent modes, `quantified` and pointer spans, and they carry shortcuts (order, first person, type-scope coupling) (R-05, R-06, R-17).

### 2.1 JTBD, ODI, VPC, OST

- **ODI job taxonomy.** A core functional job sits at the centre, with related jobs, emotional jobs (split into personal and social) and consumption-chain jobs around it. Social is therefore a sub-type of emotional, not a third sibling. [V] https://innovationroundtable.com/summit/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Strategyn_what_is_Outcome_Driven_Innovation.pdf ; https://anthonyulwick.com/2016/12/22/strategic-function-jobs-done-needs-framework/
- **ODI outcome statement.** Direction of improvement, unit of measure, object of control, contextual clarifier. Metrics seen in primary text are time, likelihood and percentage. Current vendor pages show only "minimize"; an explicit ban on "increase" was not found. [V] same whitepaper; https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done-template/
- **ODI job versus outcome.** A job is verb plus object plus clarifier, stable when solutions change. Outcomes must be solution-free and measurable; 50 to 150 per job. Importance and satisfaction come only from a survey; opportunity = importance + max(importance − satisfaction, 0). [V] whitepaper; https://strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation-process/
- **Value Proposition Canvas.** Jobs are functional, social or personal/emotional, plus supporting jobs (buyer, cocreator, transferrer). Pains are undesired outcomes, obstacles and risks, with a severity. Gains are required, expected, desired or unexpected, with a relevance. Mixing jobs and outcomes is a named common mistake. [V] https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781118968055.excerpt.pdf
- **Gains and pains are not mirror images**: "Gains are not simply the opposite of pains". [V] https://www.strategyzer.com/library/are-gains-the-opposite-of-pains
- **Christensen school.** A job is progress in particular circumstances and has functional, social and emotional dimensions at once. [V] https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/
- **Four forces.** Push, pull, anxiety, habit. Anxiety and habit are barriers to change, not pains of the current situation. [V] https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/
- **Opportunity Solution Tree.** An opportunity is an unmet need, pain point or desire, found in stories of specific past behaviour. Test for a solution in disguise: is there more than one way to address it. A child is a subset of its parent; the top level maps to moments of an experience map. Sizing asks how many customers and how often. [V] https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/ ; https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-mapping.md ; https://www.producttalk.org/sourcing-opportunities.md ; https://www.producttalk.org/prioritize-opportunities.md
- **Two schools.** Secondary sources describe jobs-as-progress against jobs-as-activities. Whether a trip is a job or a means depends on the school. No primary source with a transport example was found; the primary essays could not be fetched. [S] https://www.productfocus.com/the-jtbd-challenge/

Measured against the brief: ODI needs a direction and a unit, while `improve` holds themes and derives direction from `kind`. Only time and cost map to an ODI metric. Severity, relevance, importance and satisfaction cannot come from a quote, which the interface document already states.

### 2.2 Mobility taxonomies

- **MiD.** Five main modes: on foot, bicycle, car as driver, car as passenger, public transport. Pedelecs count as bicycle. Public transport includes taxi, long-distance coach and plane in the 2023 definition. Seven trip purposes: work, business, education, shopping, errands, leisure, escort. Regular work trips of drivers and craftsmen are a separate commercial module. [V] https://www.mobilitaet-in-deutschland.de/archive/pdf/MiD2017_Ergebnisbericht.pdf ; https://www.stmb.bayern.de/assets/stmi/vum/handlungsfelder/mid2023_langbericht_bayern.pdf
- **UK National Travel Survey.** Main mode by longest stage. Separate codes for car, motorcycle, van/lorry, several bus types, coach, underground, train, light rail, taxi/minicab, air, and newer codes for e-bike, e-scooter, mobility scooter and ferry. E-bikes stay under pedal cycle in tables. Purposes: commuting, business, education, escort, shopping, personal business, leisure groups. [V] https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2024/nts-2024-notes-and-definitions ; https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2024-technical-report/chapter-6-glossary-and-definitions
- **Eurostat.** Passenger modal split: cars, buses and coaches, trains (wider version adds air and sea). Freight: road, rail, inland waterways, then air, sea and pipelines. The passenger mobility guidelines split car driver from passenger, merge taxi with ride-hailing, list electric light scooters separately and put e-bikes under cycling. Purposes match the MiD core plus returning home. [V] https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/en/tran_hv_ms_esms.htm ; https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/3859598/10013293/KS-GQ-19-004-EN-N.pdf ; https://www.isfort.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Passenger-Mobility-Guidelines-Version_Dec-2021.pdf
- **Stakeholders.** The SUMP guidelines (1st edition) group government and authorities, businesses and operators, communities and neighbourhoods, and others; politicians, unions, user groups and transport staff are listed explicitly. [V] https://crowplatform.com/wp-content/uploads/GUIDELINES-Developing-and-implementing-a-Sustainable-urban-mobility-plan.pdf

Measured against the brief: the 15 `mode` values cover the official lists well. Open boundaries: pedelec (official sources: bicycle), e-scooter against moped, car driver against passenger (one `car`), freight rail under `train`, pipelines only under `other`. The trip-purpose core of seven values is stable across all three surveys and is a ready value list for a later typed `situation`. `actor_type` has no clear home for politicians speaking for constituents, associations speaking for members, households and self-employed drivers.

### 2.3 Inter-annotator agreement

- **Spans in web discourse.** Unitized alpha 0.60 on comments and forum posts, 0.09 on articles and blogs; a persuasive-or-not document filter reached Fleiss kappa 0.59; agreement falls with length. [V] https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.02403v2
- **Spans in clean essays.** Unitized alpha 0.77; relation kappa 0.71 to 0.74. [S] https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.07370
- **Opinion expressions (MPQA).** Overlap-based agreement 0.72 to 0.80; sentence subjectivity kappa 0.77. [V, secondary summary] https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wcohen/10-802/fixed/Wiebe_et_al%2C_Computational_Linguistics_2005.html
- **LLMs as span annotators.** Human-human F1 0.44 on error spans, LLM-human 0.18 to 0.25. Among spans judged correct by hand, hard character overlap was 24 % and soft overlap 51 %. Few-shot examples helped one model and hurt another. [V] https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08697v3
- **Exact against overlap matching.** A gap of 0.03 to 0.10 for short spans. [V] https://aclanthology.org/W17-0812.pdf
- **Temporal links.** TimeML: which pairs get a link 0.55, relation type given a link 0.77. [S] https://aclanthology.org/S07-1014/ . TimeBank-Dense type kappa 0.56 to 0.64. [V] https://2014.aclweb.org/P14-2/pdf/P14-2082.pdf . Restricting the task to start points lifted kappa to 0.84. [V] https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.07828
- **Causal and purpose links.** With explicit connectives: connective F1 0.77, type kappa 0.70, both scored only on matched connectives. [V] https://aclanthology.org/W17-0812.pdf . Semantic links with fixed entities: kappa 0.49 to 0.51. [V] https://aclanthology.org/W16-1007.pdf
- **Categories on spans.** Motive categories: pairwise agreement 0.70 to 0.91 but alpha 0.24 to 0.32 because of skew. [V] https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.06533 . Argument schemes kappa 0.48. [V, cited in the web-discourse paper]. App-review classes 0.60 to 0.70. [S] https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23452v2
- **LLM agreement as a proxy.** Models share errors; when both err, they often give the same wrong answer. [S] https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962 . In-context examples cause majority-label and recency bias. [S] https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.09690 . Inter-LLM agreement therefore measures how reproducible the guideline is, not whether labels are right.
- **Method.** Span F1 is not chance-corrected and is not comparable to kappa bands. [S] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1090460 . Kappa falls under skewed prevalence at constant raw agreement. [S] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5712640/ . Multi-label fields are usually scored per label or with a set distance (MASI). [M] https://aclanthology.org/L06-1392/
- **Own arithmetic, not sourced.** With a 73 % majority class, kappa 0.6 needs about 84 % raw agreement; a boolean at 10 % prevalence needs about 93 %. A link scored end to end needs both endpoints matched: item F1 0.7 gives about 0.49, times a conditional link agreement of 0.6 to 0.8 gives 0.29 to 0.39. The 95 % half-width of kappa 0.6 is about ±0.28 at n = 30 and ±0.16 at n = 100.

No published agreement study for jobs-to-be-done or pain-point spans was found. The task has no direct precedent.

## 4. Answers to the open items

**Distribution gaps of the examples.**

- **single 55 % against 18 %.** Mostly not a real gap. The target comes from a teacher that read the old guideline, where several occurrences counted as multiple. Under reading (a) the two figures are not comparable. Do not rebalance toward 73 % multiple. Do break the coupling of type and scope: add a routine reported for many people and an observation about one case.
- **opinion 42 % against 73 %.** Real, and it matters, because the opinion border decides the `evidence_type` kappa. Add plain asserted pains without experience, in third-person texts.
- **individual 65 % against 44 %, public_sector 9 % against 19 %.** Real, and it matters per language: English has no public_sector or society, German no worker. Add an authority with a need of its own in English and a worker in German.
- **Not listed in the provenance but larger:** item density (5.5 against 12.6 per relevant text), text length and voice. One example should be a long, noisy, third-person chunk with one or two items, and one a long empty report.
- **Fine as they are:** the kind split, the share of empties, the share of multi-sentence quotes.

**(a) Scope of one person's routine.** Keep the reading "single", and write it into `evidence_scope`: scope counts actors or cases, frequency belongs to `evidence_type`. The examples are consistent here (all routine items are single or quantified). Add the rule for collective speakers: a body speaking of its own need is one actor.

**(b) A mode named only as absent.** The examples teach both readings. Recommended: decide on the words of the quote, not on use. Every mode word in the quote counts, also when negated or missing ("kein Auto" gives car). This is the most reproducible rule and keeps the field lexical. It reverses the bracketed reading of the provenance, so e09 i2 and e12 i1 change. The alternative (only modes used or wanted) needs a judgement per item and matches e09 i1/i3 no better.

**(c) A quantity that is not a count of cases.** It was not avoided: e11 i1 "rund 300 Filialen" is quantified, while e03 i1 "zweimal am Tag" and e08 i1 "an extra hour" are single. Recommended: `quantified` only when the figure says how many actors or occurrences have the need (a number or share of people, trips, tours, complaints). Durations, distances, prices, times of day and the size of an operation do not count. e11 i1 becomes single.

**(d) The same need twice with different evidence.** Keep two items, and state the rule: a second item only when the need is stated again in a separate passage that does not overlap the first. cairn merges duplicates across and within texts; the second mention carries its own evidence.

**(e) Links have no revise band.** Score links only on pairs whose two items both references matched, raise the threshold to 0.5 and set the link floor to 0.3, which gives a band of 0.3 to 0.5. Report the end-to-end figure next to it without using it for the decision. Draw an enriched sub-sample for links (chunks in which code finds one of the rule's own connective words), since a random pilot will not reach 30 links per type.

**(f) German answers inside the English guideline.** The verbatim rule protects provenance, not the language a labeler reads. Recommended: the owner approves an English operative sentence for each of the three keys; the template prints the English one; the German original stays under the `owner` key and in the provenance. For `item_boundaries` this English text is the rule of proposal P1.

## 6. Mitigation proposals

"Changes approved text" means the owner approved the current wording and would approve a new one.

**P1. Item boundary rule** (fixes R-01, R-20, R-23). New English text for `item_boundaries`, as the operative version of the owner's sentence: "An item is one need. Its quote is the shortest run of whole clauses that states the need, including the words that mark it as a wish, fear or problem (such as 'I want', 'schön wäre', 'I worry that') and the conjunction that opens a clause. A need may run over several sentences. A cause or consequence joined with 'so', 'because', 'weil' or 'sodass' belongs to the same item unless it is a need of its own. Two needs joined by 'and' are two items. Quotes of one text never overlap. Offsets are computed by code." And in `pain_versus_negated_gain`, `doubt.kind` and `job_kind.rule`: "label both only when each has its own clause; otherwise take the kind the wording has". Trade-off: sub-clause items such as e12 i11 and e07 i3 merge into their clause, so some fine-grained jobs are lost; in return the labels fit sentence and clause units. Changes approved text: yes, and about 15 example quotes are re-cut.

**P2. Job against gain against activity** (fixes R-02, part of R-15). Add `kind.job_versus_gain`: "A job is what the actor wants to get done: it has its own verb of doing and stays the same when the means change. A wish about how well, fast, cheaply, safely or reliably it goes is a gain. A sentence that only reports what someone does, with no want, purpose, obstacle or judgement, is not an item." Add to `relevant`: "or values an outcome they have". Delete "State it, where the text allows, apart from the solution used today" (R-22). Trade-off: e07 i1, e07 i2 and e11 i1 are no longer items unless tied to a purpose (e07 i2 stays through its "um zu" clause after P1), so fewer job items for the job map; e07 i5 and e11 i2 become gains. Changes approved text: yes.

**P3. Metric sheet inside `pilot_thresholds`** (fixes R-03, R-14, R-28). Add a `definitions` block: matching (one-to-one, token IoU ≥ 0.5, after stripping edge punctuation); unit of each attribute kappa (items matched by both references; `job_kind` only where both say job); lists scored with a set-based alpha, both-empty counted; pointers as in section 5; links on matched pairs; linear weights; bootstrap intervals over chunks; "units"; pilot size per language; re-scoring on a holdout after a revision. Add rules: a `keep_always` field below its floor stops the pilot (guideline back to the owner), it is never dropped; a field is decided per language and is produced only where it passed; `drop_order` applies only when the core-does-not-pay test of the model fails. ## 7. Decisions for the owner

The first option is the recommendation.

1. **Item boundaries (P1).** (a) Whole clauses with their wish or fear words, no overlap, cause clauses stay inside. (b) Shortest phrase of the need only, no overlap. (c) Keep the German sentence alone and let the examples show it.
2. **Job, gain, activity (P2).** (a) Job = something to get done; how well it goes = gain; a bare reported activity is no item. (b) Same, but a reported routine activity is a job. (c) Keep the current definitions.
3. **Tie between a pain or gain and its job (P14).** (a) No new field: group per cairn cluster and correct the interface document. (b) Add an `about_job` link. (c) Let `situation` point at the job's words.
4. **`states_solution` (P7).** (a) Torres' test: true when only one way would satisfy the wish. (b) True only for a named new product or facility; schedule and property wishes are false. (c) Three values: none, wanted, in use.
5. **Note (a), scope of one person's routine.** (a) Single: scope counts actors or cases. (b) Multiple: several events count.
6. **Note (b), a mode named as absent.** (a) Counts: every mode word in the quote. (b) Does not count: only modes used or wanted. (c) Counts only when the need is about that mode.
7. **Note (c), quantities.** (a) Quantified only for a number or share of affected actors or occurrences. (b) Any figure in the quote.
8. **Note (d), the same need twice.** (a) Two items when stated in separate passages. (b) Only the mention with the stronger evidence.
9. **A need voiced for others (P10).** (a) The actor is whoever has the need; the speaker's body only for its own need. (b) The speaker's body is the actor whenever it says "we".
10. **Pointers (P6).** (a) Defining noun phrase, nearest occurrence before the quote, compared as strings, inside the labeled chunk. (b) Keep "shortest span" and trim the examples. (c) Label role mentions once per text and assign items to them.
11. **Links, note (e) (P4).** (a) Score on matched pairs: threshold 0.5, floor 0.3, enriched sub-sample. (b) Keep 0.4 end to end and lower the floor to 0.3. (c) Take links out of 0.2.0 labeling.
12. **Metric sheet and decision rule (P3, section 5).** (a) Adopt the definitions and proposed values, decide with intervals, add the outcome "underpowered". (b) Adopt the definitions but keep the current values. (c) Keep everything as it is.
13. **Examples (P13).** (a) Correct, reorder and grow to 16. (b) Correct and reorder, stay at 12. (c) Keep as approved.
14. **Note (f), German sentences.** (a) Approve English operative versions; German stays in the provenance. (b) Print both languages. (c) Keep German only.
15. **`improve` (P11).** (a) Remove the direction sentence and add one line per value. (b) Keep the list, add definitions, keep direction from kind. (c) Keep as approved.
