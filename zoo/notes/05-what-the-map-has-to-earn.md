[The last note](../the-gaps/) ended with the grid taken away and the map left patchy on purpose.
That turned the question around: if the map does not have to be complete, what does it have to do
to be worth drawing at all? I have an answer now, and it is not "be true". Everything in the map
so far is true, drawn only where a text says so, and that was never the hard part.

## Two different jobs

Every entry on this map comes from a text that already exists: a complaint, a forum post, an
interview transcript. Nobody went out and asked a new question to get it. [The fourth
note](../the-gaps/) named two of the three kinds of hole that follow from that: a cause that sits
in a document I have not read, and a smooth stretch that nobody wrote about because it went fine.
Closing either one takes a different instrument, reading something else or asking or counting
something new. The map does neither. It only works with what is already written down.

That is a narrower job than it sounds, and it has a name. Using what you already know to get the
best result from it now is exploitation; going out to learn something you do not yet know, at the
cost of the result you could have had now, is exploration, and an organisation (or a person) that
only does one of the two eventually fails at the other.[ref:march-1991] The map is squarely the
first kind. It exploits text that is already sitting there. When a gap needs asking or counting,
that is exploration, a different activity with its own instrument, and the map is built to say so
rather than pretend to do it with a guess.

The project has a second instrument for the other job. One of the outcomes I hold the extraction
pilot to is that a result from it has to reach the opportunity layer of an Opportunity Solution
Tree, in the hands of someone who did not build the pipeline, in under half an hour.[ref:torres-2021]
An Opportunity Solution Tree is where new options get searched for and weighed against each other,
which is exploration's job, not the map's. The map feeds it a starting point. It does not do its
work for it, and a map that tried would be guessing at exactly the kind of thing an Opportunity
Solution Tree exists to go and find out.

## The test

So completeness was the wrong bar from the start. Here is the one I now hold an entry to: draw it
only if some decision would come out differently with it on the map than without it. Not whether
it is true. Not whether it is interesting. Whether anything downstream of the map would be chosen
differently because the entry is there.

[diagram:earns-a-place]

Take the fare-zone cause from [the second note](../how-big-is-the-question/): a pain at the ticket
counter whose reason sits in tariff documents I have not read. Drawn as a named "not read" hole, it
changes a decision: whoever owns that page now has a reason to go pull the tariff documents and
check. Leave the hole off the map, and nobody goes looking, because nothing points there. The entry
earns its place by changing what gets requested next, not by being correct about where the cause
sits.

Now take a detail that is just as true and changes nothing: the colour of the paint on a platform
edge, mentioned once in a complaint in passing. Whether it is on the map or not, the same decision
follows, which is none, because nobody was going to repaint anything either way. True, sourced,
quotable, and it still does not earn a place, because no decision hangs on it being there.

## What I changed

Every entry the map keeps now has to carry the decision it would change, next to the quote and the
kind of hole it is, the way [the fourth note](../the-gaps/) already required. An entry that cannot
name one gets dropped, however well sourced it is. I went back through the current map doing this,
and about a third of the entries, mostly plain restatements of a step everyone already expects, had
nothing that followed from drawing them. They came out. Coverage never came back as a measure;
this test replaces it, and it is harder to pad, because a padded entry makes the same failure
visible in the column meant to hold its purpose.

## What the series leaves

The series opened on whose journey to draw, because an opportunity without a place is hard to act
on. It went on to the shape of the question, the order inside it, the holes that order leaves, and
now the one thing an entry has to do to belong at all. None of the five answers makes the map
complete, and none was supposed to. What they add up to is a map that only holds what somebody
would act on finding there, which is a smaller and more honest thing than a journey map usually
promises to be. The project continues past this; the series does not have to.
