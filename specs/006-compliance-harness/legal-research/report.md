# Commercial status, personal data and teacher terms decide compliance

> **Not legal advice.** This report synthesises research notes compiled on 2026-10-08 from statutes, court summaries, regulator guidance and law-firm commentary. Several primary texts (EUR-Lex, the EDPB site, some German statutes) could not be fetched during the research and rest on secondary sources; these places are marked. Confidence labels (High / Medium / Low) give the research team's view of how firm each conclusion is, not a guarantee. Before you rely on any conclusion marked Medium or Low, or on anything that touches the already-published scout-large model, have a German lawyer for IT, copyright and data-protection law check it.

**Bottom line.** For a private, non-monetised open-source model zoo, the EU AI Act, the Cyber Resilience Act, the new product liability rules and the German Impressum duty mostly fall away. They all turn on two things: not trading commercially and not marketing a model for a high-risk or safety purpose. Three regimes apply in full, and the compliance harness must be built around them. The first is the **GDPR**: the household exemption does not cover anything published to the internet ([Lindqvist summary](https://www.thomashelbing.com/en/wissen/dsgvo-hub/rechtsprechung/1.4.43-eugh-lindqvist)), so the owner is a full controller for scraped texts, for the labelling calls to LLM providers and for every published artefact. The second is **copyright, TDM and database law**: §44b UrhG requires lawful access, respect for machine-readable opt-outs and deletion of copies once they are no longer needed ([§44b UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__44b.html)), and memorisation or verbatim output is outside the TDM exception under LG München I's first-instance ruling. The third is **licence and contract law**: CC BY/BY-SA attribution, ShareAlike for the MIMII model, and the teacher providers' "no competing models" clauses. For scout-large, the riskiest points are not the AI Act. They are the following. Teacher and reference labelling sent parliamentary and interview texts, which name people, to OpenRouter providers "chosen per request" with no pinned provider or zero-retention flag. Claude reference labels were produced through a consumer subscription via the Claude Code CLI. The repository has no public privacy notice, NOTICE file or SECURITY.md. Several legal questions remain open on 2026-10-08: the BGH decision in LAION (announced for 17.12.2026), the appeal in GEMA v OpenAI, CJEU C-250/25, the GDPR "Art. 88bis" omnibus, and final CRA guidance. The harness therefore needs a legal-reference freshness check and dated re-review triggers, not only static rules.

## One fact switches off five regimes: no commercial activity

Most product-style EU regimes attach to "making available on the market", which means supply "in the course of a commercial activity, whether in return for payment or free of charge" ([AI Act Art. 3](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/3/)). The CRA says this outright for open source: FOSS "not monetised by their manufacturers should not be considered to be a commercial activity" ([CRA recital 18](https://prighter.com/resources/laws/cyber-resilience-act/recitals/recital-18)). The German product-liability bill excludes "Open-Source-Software, die außerhalb einer Geschäftstätigkeit entwickelt oder bereitgestellt wird" ([Bundestag hib](https://www.bundestag.de/presse/hib/kurzmeldungen-1150966)). §5 DDG covers only "geschäftsmäßige" digital services ([IHK München](https://www.ihk-muenchen.de/de/Service/Recht-und-Steuern/Internetrecht/Impressum-im-Internet/)). The AI Act's open-source carve-outs disappear once a model is "provided against a price or otherwise monetised, including through the provision of technical support" ([Recital 103](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/recital/103/)). A single harness guard against monetisation therefore protects five regimes at once. It is the most valuable automated check in the whole design.

The donation question is the open edge of the monetisation test. Voluntary donations and sponsorships are described as non-commercial in the draft CRA guidance ([Open Source For You](https://www.opensourceforu.com/2026/07/eu-clarifies-cra-rules-for-non-commercial-open-source/)), which the Commission approved as a draft on 27 July 2026 as C(2026) 5252 ([cyberresilienceact.eu](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/commission-guidance.html)). Secondary sources disagree on whether paid consulting around free software is commercial ([CRA Evidence](https://craevidence.com/blog/cra-commission-guidance-march-2026)). The AI Act GPAI guidelines do not list donations as monetisation ([GPAI guidelines overview](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/gpai-guidelines-overview/)). The harness should therefore block paid tiers, paid APIs and dual licences outright, and route any FUNDING.yml or sponsor link to an owner decision. The repository currently has no FUNDING file.

## AI Act: none of the models is GPAI or high-risk, and wording keeps it that way

**Applicability (Medium-High).** None of the models is a general-purpose AI model. Under the Commission's guidelines a model is presumed GPAI if it was trained with more than 10²³ FLOP and can generate language, images or video. Narrow models such as transcription or weather models are excluded even above that threshold ([GPAI guidelines overview](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/gpai-guidelines-overview/)). scout-large is an XLM-R-large token classifier that does not generate. The research team estimates XLM-R pretraining at about 2×10²² FLOP and the fine-tune at about 10¹⁷ FLOP. This is their own calculation, not a published figure, so the harness should record it with its assumptions. The deployable artefacts (scout-large with inference code, the CAN IDS firmware, the MIMII and HAR models) most likely meet the Art. 3(1) definition of an "AI system", because they infer predictions from inputs ([Art. 3](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/3/)). The Art. 2(12) open-source exclusion then very likely takes them out of the Regulation: it applies to systems "released under free and open-source licences, unless they are placed on the market or put into service as high-risk AI systems or as an AI system that falls under Article 5 or 50" ([Art. 2](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/)). The research exclusions in Art. 2(6) and 2(8) do not cover public releases intended for others to use. They cover only internal runs before release.

Whether a private individual uploading for free counts as acting "in the course of a commercial activity" is unsettled for AI systems. For GPAI models, the Commission says the uploader to a repository is the provider ([GPAI guidelines overview](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/gpai-guidelines-overview/)). The prudent course is to treat the owner as provider and rely on Art. 2(12).

**High-risk risk sits in the CAN IDS and in card wording (Medium-High that none is high-risk).** Recital 55 says that "components intended to be used solely for cybersecurity purposes should not qualify as safety components" ([Recital 55](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/recital/55/)). The adopted Digital Omnibus narrowed "safety component" to systems whose intended purpose is preventing or mitigating health and safety risks. It also moved Annex III obligations to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I obligations to 2 Aug 2028 ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes)). Vehicle type-approval law (Regulations 2018/858 and 2019/2144) sits in Annex I Section B. Only Art. 6(1), 102–109 and 112 of the AI Act apply to those products ([Art. 2](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/)), so AI requirements reach vehicles only through future type-approval acts addressed to manufacturers. The counter-argument is real. An IDS that actively blocks frames or triggers a safe state has failure modes that could endanger safety, so the published intended purpose must stay "detection, monitoring and research reference, not an active safety mechanism". The same logic applies to the other models. MIMII could become a machinery safety component if it were marketed as a safety function. HAR would fall under Annex III point 4 if marketed for employee monitoring. scout-large would be caught if marketed for recruitment or worker evaluation. scout-large's published out-of-scope list already excludes "decisions about individuals; profiling or identifying the authors of texts" ([scout-large card preview](../../../reports/scout-large/model-card-preview-0.1.1.md)), which is the right lever.

**Transparency and literacy (High).** Art. 50 does not apply, because no model is a chatbot or generates synthetic content. Art. 50 obligations apply from 2 Aug 2026, with a grace period to 2 Dec 2026 for Art. 50(2) marking ([Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html?id=102nbon)). Any future generative scout head would end the Art. 2(12) exclusion and trigger both Art. 50 and a GPAI review, so the harness needs a "generative capability" flag. The omnibus softened Art. 4 AI literacy to "take measures to support" ([KI-Campus](https://ki-campus.org/en/blog/eu-ai-act-ai-omnibus-recalibrates-article-4-ai-literacy-remains-mandatory)). For a one-person project without employees, usage guidance in the model card is enough.

**Voluntary GPAI-style documentation is cheap insurance.** If a future model did become GPAI, the open-source exemption would still leave the copyright policy and the public training-content summary in place (Art. 53(1)(c), (d)) ([Art. 53](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/53/)). The AI Office template asks for sources, the top 10% of scraped domains, the crawl period, and synthetic data together with the models that generated it ([WilmerHale](https://wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/european-commission-releases-mandatory-template-for-public-disclosure-of-ai-training-data)). The register can fill every field mechanically. Generating the summary per release costs little and makes a later GPAI release painless. A licence such as CC BY-NC on any model artefact would break both Art. 2(12) and Art. 53(2), so the harness must block NC and ND licences ([GPAI guidelines overview](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/gpai-guidelines-overview/)).

**Automated checks:** OSI-compatible licence with no NC/ND; weights and usage information public and not paid; monetisation guard; card lint against Annex III and safety terms (recruitment, credit, biometric, emotion, ASIL, "vehicle safety"); compute estimate with an alert at 3.3×10²² and 10²³ FLOP; generative-head flag; trivial-baseline delta recorded as evidence against "simple prediction system"; training-content summary generated; legal-reference freshness. **Human decisions:** a signed classification record per model covering AI system vs model-only, GPAI, high-risk, safety component and monetisation status, with re-review triggers (new modality, more than 1B parameters, new intended use, an automotive deployment partner).

## GDPR is the regime that actually binds, starting with the labelling calls

**Applicability (High).** The household exemption is read strictly. Publishing data on the internet to an indefinite audience takes an activity outside it ([Lindqvist](https://www.thomashelbing.com/en/wissen/dsgvo-hub/rechtsprechung/1.4.43-eugh-lindqvist); [Ryneš](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/cjeu-adopts-strict-approach-use-cctv)). The owner is a controller under the GDPR and a "nicht-öffentliche Stelle" under the BDSG ([BDSG §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bdsg_2018/__1.html)). Non-commercial status does not change this.

**Legal basis and model anonymity (High on the current position).** The realistic basis for scraping and training is legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f), shown through the three-step test in EDPB Opinion 28/2024. That opinion treats a model as anonymous only if extracting personal data from it is "negligible", assessed case by case with documented evidence. It lists opt-outs "from the outset" and transparency beyond Arts. 13/14 as mitigations, and names dataset erasure and retraining as remedies for unlawful development ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/edpb-opinion-28-2024-key-takeaways-on-processing-personal-data-in-the-context-of-ai-models)). The CNIL accepts scraping for AI on legitimate interest, subject to robots.txt and ai.txt compliance, exclusion of sensitive data and a DPIA where risk is high ([Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2025/06/web-scraping-for-ai-development--the-cnil-builds-on-edpb-guidanc.html)). The proposed statutory AI legitimate interest (Art. 88c, renamed "88bis" in a leaked Council draft) is not law. As of September 2026 the Council has no general approach ([Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-09-21-noyb-gdpr-article-88bis-ai/)). Because scout-large is an encoder that outputs spans of the user's input, extraction through queries is structurally hard. The research team still advises against claiming "the model is anonymous". The card should say "measures taken to minimise personal data", backed by a recorded memorisation or membership-inference test.

**Special categories (Medium-High).** Forum and review texts will contain health, religion, political and sex-life data. Art. 9(2)(e) requires the data subject's own deliberate act of making data public ([Meta v Bundeskartellamt](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2023/07/eu-cjeus-landmark-decision-in-meta-vs-bundeskartellamt/)). It never covers third parties, and it cannot be verified post by post. §27 BDSG research processing is a weak fit for a private individual ([BDSG §27](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bdsg_2018/__27.html)). The model's purpose does not need sensitive content, so data minimisation argues for a sensitive-topic quarantine before labelling. Accessibility and disability pains are the hard case. They are both health data and highly relevant to mobility, and keeping them needs an explicit, documented owner decision.

**Transparency (High).** Art. 14 applies because the data does not come from the data subjects. Individual notice is disproportionate, but Art. 14(5)(b) expressly requires "making the information publicly available" ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). In practice a public training-data privacy notice is mandatory. It must cover the controller contact, purposes and legal basis, data categories, source domains, recipients (Anthropic, OpenRouter and its upstream providers, Hugging Face), third-country transfers, retention per stage, and an Art. 21 objection right stated separately. Under SRB, recipients are judged from the controller's view at the time of collection, so the LLM providers must be named ([Clifford Chance on SRB](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2025/09/pseudonymized-data-after-edps-v-srb.html)). The repository contains no PRIVACY file today.

**LLM providers are the sharpest GDPR exposure (Medium-High).** Since 28 Sept 2025, Anthropic's consumer plans (Free, Pro and Max, including Claude Code) use chats for training unless the user opts out, with retention of up to five years. API and commercial plans are excluded from this ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updates-to-our-consumer-terms)). Under consumer terms Anthropic is not the owner's processor. Sending third-party text there is a disclosure to another controller. OpenRouter routes to upstream providers whose location and data policies vary. Some are outside the EU and the Data Privacy Framework. US transfers can rely on the DPF, which the General Court upheld in Latombe (T-553/23, 3 Sept 2025) ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2025/09/03/transatlantic-trouble-not-this-time-latombes-challenge-to-eu-us-data-transfers-fails)), but only where the recipient is certified. Whether Latombe was appealed was not confirmed. The project's own configuration shows teacher candidates on OpenRouter with `provider_order: null` ("provider chosen by OpenRouter") ([models.yaml](../../../configs/productdev/jtbd/models.yaml)). That means the transfer destination of each labelling call is unknown after the fact.

**Retention, rights and records (Medium).** Raw snapshots remain fully searchable personal data until deleted. Shortening their retention, for example to 90 days or until redaction QA has passed (the researchers' proposal, not a legal rule), is the main lever. It aligns with the copyright deletion duty discussed below. Redacted working copies strengthen the Art. 11 position. A rights process should work as follows: search by URL or quote across all stores, delete, add a hashed entry to a permanent suppression list, probe the current model, and reply within one month. A DPIA is not clearly mandatory but is advisable, given large-scale scraping, likely Art. 9 content and novel technology. The Art. 30(5) exemption for small organisations does not apply, because the processing is not occasional and includes special categories ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). Note the edge case: a *mandatory* DPIA would, in theory, trigger the §38 BDSG DPO duty ([BDSG §38](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bdsg_2018/__38.html)). The DSK's June 2025 TOM guidance for AI development is the best German checklist for development-phase controls, although it expressly excludes the legality of scraping ([DSK OH KI-Systeme](https://www.datenschutz-berlin.de/fileadmin/user_upload/pdf/publikationen/DSK/2025/20250617-DSK-OH_KI-Systeme.pdf)).

**Sensor and vehicle data (Medium).** UCI HAR is pseudonymous, not anonymous. Studies show motion data re-identifies individuals at high rates ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10358383)). Training on it is low risk; *re-hosting* it makes the owner a distributor. The EDPB treats journey details, driving style and technical wear data as personal data relating to the driver ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/how-much-can-your-car-know-about-you-eu-guidelines-on-data-protection-and-connected-vehicles)). ROAD and can-train-and-test therefore need a scan for VINs, GPS signals and absolute timestamps before any republication. Nobody has inspected the contents of these datasets yet.

**Automated checks:** pre-send PII scan, with no unredacted text sent to any non-local model; a provider allowlist carrying DPA, training opt-out, ZDR and DPF/EU flags; OpenRouter calls pinned to allowed providers; sensitive-category quarantine; suppression list applied at fetch, label and train; provenance for every training example; retention TTL assertions; memorisation or membership-inference test per release; n-gram overlap and PII scan of every published artefact; privacy notice present, linked from every card, and diffed against the registry domains and the providers in the logs; Art. 30 record consistent with configs; VIN/GPS scan for vehicle data; rights-register deadlines. **Human decisions:** accept controller status and publish a contact; approve an LIA per source class; decide on the DPIA (and with it the §38 question); choose provider tiers; set retention periods; decide on sensitive-but-relevant content; set thresholds; decide on mirroring human-subject datasets.

## Copyright allows mining but not memorising, and opt-outs are judged at fetch time

**Legal basis (High on the text, Medium on classification).** §44b UrhG is open to anyone. It permits copies of lawfully accessible works for TDM. The copies "must be deleted once no longer needed", and online reservations count only "in maschinenlesbarer Form" ([§44b UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__44b.html)). §60d covers "einzelne Forscher, sofern sie nicht kommerzielle Zwecke verfolgen". It has no opt-out and longer retention, but it does not allow public release of the copies ([§60d UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__60d.html)). OLG Hamburg let LAION, a non-profit research association, rely on §60d despite downstream commercial use ([Kanzlei Plutte](https://www.ra-plutte.de/olg-hamburg-erlaubt-fotonutzung-fuer-ki-training/amp/)). For a lone developer whose stated aim is commercially usable models, that is a weak analogy. §44b should be the primary basis, with §60d at most a secondary argument.

**What counts as an opt-out is unsettled (Medium-High on the OLG holding, Low on the BGH outcome).** OLG Hamburg (10.12.2025, 5 U 104/24) held that a natural-language reservation was not machine-readable in 2021. It left open whether natural language can ever qualify, judged effectiveness at the moment of use, and put the burden of proof on the rightsholder ([itmr-legal](https://itmr-legal.de/blog/ki-training-widersprechen-tdm-vorbehalt); [urheber.info](https://urheber.info/diskurs/hamburger-olg-urteil-im-fall-laion)). The revision is pending as BGH I ZR 281/25, with a decision reportedly announced for 17.12.2026. That date comes from a single source that also misdates the OLG judgment, so treat it with caution. Recognised signals are robots.txt (RFC 9309) and TDMRep, a W3C Community Group report, not a Recommendation, which uses `tdm-reservation` in `/.well-known/tdmrep.json`, HTTP headers or meta tags ([W3C TDMRep](https://www.w3.org/community/reports/tdmrep/CG-FINAL-tdmrep-20240510/)). The conservative policy treats all of the following as reservations: AI-specific user-agent blocks, `noai` meta tags, ai.txt, and natural-language ToS clauses on post-2025 pages. Signals must be snapshotted at fetch time, and each new training run is a new use, so opt-outs have to be re-evaluated before every run.

**Memorisation and verbatim output (Medium, not final).** LG München I (GEMA v OpenAI, 11.11.2025) held that a model reproducing a work recognisably contains a §16 reproduction. It also held that the TDM exception "does not permit memorisation in the AI language model or regurgitation in the output" ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/deu/legal-updates/gema-vs.-openai-urheberrechtliche-grundsatzentscheidung-des-landgerichts-muenchen-i-ergangen)). OpenAI announced an appeal. CJEU C-250/25 (Like Company v Google) will address output that is partially identical to source text ([DDG](https://en.ddg.fr/actualite/first-ai-related-reference-to-the-cjeu-preliminary-questions-in-case-c-250-25-like-company-v-google-ireland)). scout-large's design is on the safe side of this line: every `quote` "is always a verbatim span of the input" ([scout-large card preview](../../../reports/scout-large/model-card-preview-0.1.1.md)). The verbatim text therefore comes from the user's document at inference time, not from the model's memory. The residual risks sit in published cards, evaluation reports and examples. Quotations there need a genuine §51 purpose, attribution and an unchanged text. The harness should enforce a rule: no span of the raw corpus longer than N characters in any published artefact, unless the source is a §5 official work or carries a compatible licence with attribution.

**Database right and the compendium (Medium-High).** Scraping a substantial part of a forum or repository is extraction under §87b, but §87c(1) extends TDM to database rights, with source attribution ([§87c UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__87c.html)). Republishing substantial parts is not covered. The CJEU in CV-Online v Melons treats making the contents of a database available as re-utilisation where it harms the maker's investment ([Clifford Chance](https://cliffordchance.com/expertise/services/intellectual-property/global-ip-updates/2021/q4/cv-online-latvia-v-melons.html)). A Hugging Face compendium of links, metadata and licence information in the owner's own words is low risk. Re-hosting is acceptable only where each record's licence allows redistribution. Where no sui generis right exists, contractual ToS can bind independently ([Osborne Clarke on Ryanair](https://www.osborneclarke.com/de/insights/besser-keinen-gesetzlichen-datenbankschutz-eugh-lasst-vertragliche-beschrankungen-von-datenbanknutzung-zu-wenn-die-datenbank-nicht-gesetzlich-geschutzt-ist)).

**Official works (Medium).** Laws and decisions are free under §5(1). Bundestag Plenarprotokolle are generally treated as §5(2) works that may be reproduced unchanged with the source credited ([§5 UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__5.html)). Wissenschaftliche Dienste papers, commissioned studies and foreign parliaments' licences need a decision per document series.

**Automated checks:** a fetch gate for robots.txt, TDMRep, `noai` tags, ai.txt, auth, paywalls, CAPTCHAs and piracy or denylisted domains, where any hit means skip and log; a ToS classifier whose hits default to exclusion pending review; a provenance record per document that survives deletion of the content; a retention purpose and review date per snapshot; opt-out re-check per training run; corpus-overlap and memorisation gates; a published copyright policy (crawler user-agent, signals honoured, takedown contact) that mirrors the GPAI Code's copyright chapter ([Slaughter and May](https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102ktcs/mining-the-copyright-chapter-of-the-gpai-code)). **Human decisions:** §44b only or also §60d; whether to treat natural-language ToS as binding (recommended yes); the verbatim-span threshold and retention period; §5 classification per series; link vs re-host for the compendium; how to handle opt-outs that arrive after training, which is unsettled law.

## Licences and teacher terms turn data choices into model-licence choices

**CC datasets (Medium).** Creative Commons does not say whether trained weights are "Adapted Material". Its position is that the licence applies only where copyright permission is needed. It recommends following the licence conditions conservatively, accepts a link to the dataset as attribution for training, and says models "based on ShareAlike content" that are shared publicly should use the same licence ([CC AI training guide](https://creativecommons.org/using-cc-licensed-works-for-ai-training-2/)). BY-SA §3(b) requires CC BY-SA 4.0 or a compatible licence and forbids added terms or technical measures that restrict reuse ([CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)). In practice, the MIMII model weights must be CC BY-SA 4.0 and must not be gated with extra terms. The code in the same repository can stay Apache-2.0 if per-file licensing is explicit (REUSE). Models trained only on CC BY data (ROAD, can-train-and-test, UCI HAR) can be Apache-2.0 but must carry full TASL attribution: title, author, source and licence, plus a modification note. The 4.0 licences also license sui generis database rights ([CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)). Licence breaches end the licence automatically, with a 30-day cure window. Platform licence labels are often wrong: the Data Provenance Initiative audited more than 1,800 datasets and found widespread omissions and mislabelling ([arXiv 2310.16787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16787)). The harness must verify each licence at the original publisher and record any mismatch.

| Training inputs | Apache-2.0 model | CC BY-SA 4.0 model | Duties |
|---|---|---|---|
| MIT base (XLM-R) | yes | yes | MIT notice |
| + CC BY 4.0 data | yes | yes | TASL + modification note |
| + CC BY-SA 4.0 data (MIMII) | **no** (conservative) | **required** | TASL, same licence, no gating |
| + labels from closed LLMs | contract question | contract question | provider ToS sign-off |
| + labels from MIT open-weight LLM | yes | yes | MIT notice (conservative) |

**Code and firmware (High on texts).** Apache-2.0 §4 requires a copy of the licence, prominent notices on modified files, retention of existing notices, and propagation of any NOTICE file ([Apache 2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0)). emlearn is MIT-licensed, and its README says nothing about generated C code ([emlearn](https://github.com/emlearn/emlearn)). Firmware that compiles emlearn's runtime headers should therefore carry the MIT notice, plus a data attribution in the header comment. A fine-tune contains "substantial portions" of xlm-roberta-large, so its MIT notice must ship with scout-large ([MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit)). Hugging Face shows `license`, `base_model` and `datasets` (Hub IDs only) in the YAML header. It has no TASL field, so attribution belongs in the card text and a NOTICE file ([HF model cards](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/en/model-cards)).

**Teacher and reference terms are the highest contract risk (Medium-High on clause texts, unsettled on "competing").** Anthropic's Consumer Terms, which cover Pro and Max subscriptions, forbid using the services "to develop any products or services that compete with our Services, including to develop or train any artificial intelligence or machine learning algorithms or models". They also forbid automated access without an API key ([Anthropic Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). The Commercial Terms bar training "competing AI models" ([Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)). OpenRouter hands output rights to each model's "Model Terms" and makes the user "solely responsible" for reviewing them ([OpenRouter Terms](https://openrouter.ai/terms)). OpenAI forbids using Output "to develop models that compete with OpenAI" ([OpenAI ToU](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/)). Google's Gemini API terms have an equivalent clause ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). MiMo weights are MIT-licensed ([HF XiaomiMiMo](https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.5-DFlash)). Whether a narrow mobility span classifier "competes" with Claude or GPT is arguable rather than settled. These restrictions bind only the account holder, not downstream users, but a breach is still the owner's risk.

**Automated checks:** licence allowlist (CC-BY-4.0, CC-BY-SA-4.0, CC0, MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD), with NC and ND blocking; ShareAlike propagation rule; required TASL fields; NOTICE completeness for MIT, Apache and BSD inputs; HF YAML equal to the manifest; `reuse lint` ([REUSE](https://reuse.software/spec-3.3/)); no gating on BY-SA repos; teacher entries whose access path is consumer, a closed API or a closed OpenRouter route are blocked until a ToS sign-off is recorded; terms snapshots older than N months trigger re-review. **Human decisions:** consumer Claude vs API vs open-weight teachers; accepting OpenRouter closed models; adopting the "weights equal Adapted Material" reading (recommended); asking emlearn about the licence of generated code.

## Security, export, liability and Impressum are documentation duties

**CRA (High while non-monetised).** The project is neither a manufacturer nor an open-source steward. Stewards are legal persons, a reading the research team holds at Medium-High confidence without having verified the article text. The 11 Sep 2026 reporting duty and full application on 11 Dec 2027 therefore do not attach ([CRA Evidence](https://craevidence.com/blog/cra-commission-guidance-march-2026)). Integrators that use the code take on the CRA duties themselves and may report vulnerabilities upstream. A SECURITY.md with a contact point meets that expectation, and GitHub also recommends one for dual-use content ([GitHub AUP](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-active-malware-or-exploits)). The repository has none today. An optional CycloneDX SBOM for firmware builds is a goodwill measure.

**Dual-use (Medium-High).** Defensive CAN anomaly classifiers do not evade detection, extract data or alter execution paths. They are therefore not "intrusion software" under Regulation 2021/821, as amended by Delegated Regulation 2025/2003 ([EUR-Lex 2025/2003](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2025/2003/oj/eng)). Openly published technology is "in the public domain" under the EU notes ([Liverpool export guidance](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/legal/exportcontrols/exemptionsfortheacademiccommunity/)) and "published" under US EAR §734.7 ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/subtitle-B/chapter-VII/subchapter-C/part-734/section-734.7)). Approval-based gating on Hugging Face could weaken that published status. Three rules follow. Publish attack-type research descriptively. Never share attack research privately with non-EU recipients before publication. Avoid non-standard cryptography. Hugging Face prohibits content "designed to... gain unauthorized access to systems" ([HF Content Policy](https://huggingface.co/content-policy)). Turnkey injection tools against named vehicles should therefore stay out of the zoo, and real vulnerabilities should go through coordinated disclosure.

**Product liability and disclaimers (High for the exemption).** The ProdHaftG bill (BT-Drs. 21/4297) exempts non-commercial FOSS and is planned to apply from 9 Dec 2026 ([Drucksache 21/4297](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/042/2104297.pdf)). As of August 2026 the final votes were pending. Free OSS is treated as a gift, so liability is limited to intent and gross negligence under §521 BGB ([exali](https://exali.de/Info-Base/open-source)). Apache §§7–8 are not fully enforceable in Germany, and no German court has ruled on them. Honest limitations sections are therefore the real liability control. "Production-ready for vehicles" despite known flaws is exactly the kind of statement that turns into gross negligence. UNECE R155/R156 and ISO/SAE 21434 bind OEMs, not this project. The CAN cards should still disclaim them: not developed under ISO/SAE 21434 or ISO 26262, no type-approval claims. This last point rests on the researchers' background knowledge and was not fetched.

**Impressum (Medium-Low).** §5 DDG is very likely not triggered while the project is non-commercial ([e-recht24](https://e-recht24.de/artikel/datenschutz/209.html)). The §18(1) MStV exemption for "exclusively personal or family purposes" is doubtful for an outward-facing model zoo. The GDPR privacy notice needs a controller contact anyway. A single minimal contact page with a name and a service (c/o) address, linked from the HF org card and GitHub, settles both duties without exposing a home address.

## What the already-published scout-large needs now

scout-large 0.1.0 and 0.1.1 were published on 2026-10-07. Its provenance is better than the brief assumed. The 0.1.1 card lists mostly parliamentary records (Bundestag §5, UK Open Parliament Licence, Oireachtas CC BY, US public-domain hearings, European Parliament, Senedd, Scottish Parliament), two CC BY papers and CC BY "anonymised" Zenodo interview transcripts. It states that "forum and review texts were not available for training", that a single teacher (MiMo V2.6 Pro, MIT) produced the labels, and that the training data is not published ([scout-large card preview](../../../reports/scout-large/model-card-preview-0.1.1.md)). This lowers the copyright, opt-out and Art. 9 exposure considerably. Five concrete gaps remain.

First, **GDPR transfers.** Parliamentary records name MPs and witnesses, so they are personal data even though they are public documents. The MiMo teacher ran on OpenRouter with the provider "chosen per request". The reference labels went to OpenAI through OpenRouter and to Claude through the Claude Code CLI subscription. The owner cannot currently show where those texts went or under what retention, and no privacy notice names these recipients. Second, **Claude consumer terms.** The Claude reference labels shape the benchmark used to select and claim quality for the model. Whether "develop... models" covers evaluation, and whether scripted CLI use counts as "automated access", is an interpretation risk. Moving reference labelling to the API, or documenting a risk acceptance, closes it. The training labels themselves came from the MIT-licensed MiMo, which is favourable, but OpenRouter's hosting provider's terms also applied to those calls. Third, **attribution.** The card lists source and licence per document but not TASL elements, modification notes or licence-required attribution statements. There is no NOTICE file in the repository with the XLM-R MIT notice. These are cure-within-30-days items under CC §6 ([CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)) and are cheap to fix in 0.1.2. Fourth, **the Zenodo interview transcripts** are human-subject data. Their "anonymised" label and consent basis should be recorded in the register. Fifth, **raw snapshot retention** needs a recorded purpose and deletion date under both §44b(2) and Art. 5(1)(e). None of these requires withdrawing the model. Its encoder design, its extractive quotes and its mostly public-sector corpus keep the risk low. All five should be fixed in the next patch release, with a dated note in the card.

## Harness specification: one register, fail-closed gates, generated artefacts

The design principle comes from all six research strands. Keep one machine-readable register as the source of truth. Generate every public compliance text from it. Treat missing evidence as a failure. HF YAML stays the published surface. The register keys align with the SPDX 3.0 Dataset profile (`anonymizationMethodUsed`, `hasSensitivePersonalInformation`, `dataCollectionProcess`, `intendedUse`, `knownBias`) so that an SPDX or CycloneDX export is a mechanical transform ([arXiv 2504.16743](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.16743)). Review cadence is every release and at least every six months, matching the AI Office template's update rhythm ([WilmerHale](https://wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/european-commission-releases-mandatory-template-for-public-disclosure-of-ai-training-data)). Release documentation is frozen per tag, which makes the CoP-style 10-year retention free ([CoP Transparency](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/en/cop-transparency)).

### Checks in fail-closed order

The order runs from the cheapest and most fundamental checks to publication. Data that should never have been collected is stopped before it costs labelling money or leaves the machine. A failure at any stage stops all later stages. A missing record counts as a failure.

| # | Stage | Check (fails on) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Meta | Register schema validation; any `next_review < today`; legal-reference versions older than N months; expired waivers |
| 1 | Fetch | Suppression list or domain denylist or piracy list hit; auth, paywall or CAPTCHA; robots.txt disallow (project UA, `*`, AI UAs); TDMRep reservation; `noai`/X-Robots-Tag; ai.txt; ToS classifier hit without human confirmation |
| 2 | Ingest | Provenance record incomplete; licence not on the allowlist or primary licence mismatch unresolved; §5 series undecided; human-subject dataset without consent/ethics field; vehicle data VIN/GPS/timestamp scan hit without acceptance |
| 3 | Pre-send | Redaction not run on 100% of the batch; residual PII scan hit; Art. 9 classifier hit not quarantined; provider not on the allowlist (DPA / opt-out / ZDR / DPF-EU flags); OpenRouter call without a pinned provider |
| 4 | Retention | Raw snapshot older than TTL; snapshot without purpose or review date; deletion not logged |
| 5 | Train | Opt-outs not re-evaluated since last run; suppression hits in the training set; any `-SA-` input with a non-BY-SA weights licence; NC/ND/unknown licence; teacher without ToS sign-off |
| 6 | Model | Canary completion or memorisation over threshold; membership-inference above threshold; compute estimate missing or above 3.3×10²² FLOP without review; generative head present without review; trivial-baseline delta missing |
| 7 | Artefacts | Corpus n-gram or character-span overlap over threshold in card, README, examples, eval files or Space (except §5 or attributed CC); PII scan hit; examples not tagged synthetic with generation provenance; eval reports with per-example scraped text |
| 8 | Card lint | Missing intended use, out-of-scope, limitations, dual-use (security repos), automotive standards disclaimer (CAN), export note, privacy notice link, copyright policy link; Annex III or safety-function terms; marketing claims ("production-ready", "certified"); "anonymous model" claim |
| 9 | Licence artefacts | `reuse lint`; NOTICE / THIRD_PARTY / card attribution not byte-identical to the rendered output; HF `license` or `base_model` differs from the manifest; BY-SA repo gated |
| 10 | Repo hygiene | LICENSE or SECURITY.md missing; gitleaks hit; monetisation markers (block) or FUNDING links (owner gate); firmware SBOM missing; file under an attack path without owner sign-off |
| 11 | Notices | Privacy notice does not list every registry domain and every provider in the labelling logs; retention in the notice differs from the TTL config; Art. 30 record inconsistent with configs |
| 12 | Sign-off | Classification record or release approval by the owner missing |

## Conclusion

The first lesson is that the regime that sounds most relevant, the AI Act, is the one this project can most easily stay out of. It stays out through two disciplines the harness can enforce: no monetisation, and careful intended-purpose wording. The regimes that do bind (GDPR, §44b UrhG, CC licences, provider contracts) attach to *process*, not to the model. They concern where texts were sent, what was kept and for how long, and what appears verbatim in public. That is why the harness has to live at fetch, pre-send and publication time, and not only at release. scout-large shows the pattern clearly. Its weights are low-risk by design, while its exposure lies in un-pinned OpenRouter routing, a consumer-plan Claude reference, and missing notice files. All of these can be fixed in a patch release without retraining.

The second lesson is that much of the legal ground will move before mid-2027. The BGH in LAION, the GEMA appeal and CJEU C-250/25 will define what counts as an opt-out and whether memorisation is reproduction. The GDPR omnibus may create a statutory AI legitimate interest with an unconditional objection right. Final CRA guidance will fix the donation and consulting edge. A harness that records signals at fetch time, version-stamps its legal references and fails when a review date passes will adapt to those rulings by configuration rather than by rework. That makes the investment worthwhile even for a one-person project.
